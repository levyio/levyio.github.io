    Why the measurement reads high measured

    Box-counting at finite resolution is biased: a smooth line measures —, not 1.000, on this exact pipeline. That bias is measured, on screen, and applies to every number above — it is why Takagi reads 1.086 against a theorem that says exactly 1, and why the Weierstrass measurements sit a few hundredths below their predictions. A tool that hid this would be lying with true numbers.

    What this is

    Takagi's function is continuous everywhere, differentiable nowhere, and visibly rough — and its box dimension is exactly 1, by theorem. It is not a fractal. Nearly every claim that sound "makes fractals" rests on the error this page exists to make impossible: roughness is not fractality, and the only way to know is to measure.

    Every count here is asserted against the same pipeline run in numpy (docs/verify_roughness.py) — all 49 integer box counts, exactly. The Weierstrass presets, Riemann, Takagi and both controls carry golden values; moving the sliders leaves the validated method on unvalidated parameters, which is what an instrument is for.

    The failure path is shipped: ?fault=prediction, ?fault=counts, ?fault=golden.
